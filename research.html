The screener, tested on its own history

Does the ranking predict returns?

Before trusting a ranking, ask whether it would have worked. This page tests the screener's weighting, and 26 alternatives from the investing literature, on fifteen years of S&P 500 history rebuilt from the financial statements companies had actually published at each date.

  • Not measurably. The ranking's average monthly rank correlation with the next month's return was −0.003 over 2012–2019 and 0.000 over 2020–2026. Both are zero within the noise.
  • No alternative did better. None of the 26 alternative weightings cleared the bar set before the test, in either period.
  • The test can see a signal when there is one. Fed information it could not have had at the time (today's filings, for past months), the same test finds a strong one.

How it was tested

Test enough ideas on one history and some look excellent by luck. The test was set up to prevent that:

  1. 1Every idea, its source and its predicted result were written down and published in the project's history before it was run.
  2. 2Ideas were compared with the current weighting month by month, on the same stocks, and judged on months they were never tuned on.
  3. 3The years 2012–2019 were set aside as an untouched test, with the rule for passing it, before any of that data was built.
  4. 4A result counts only if it clears a bar that allows for how many ideas were tried: p < 0.05 / 27 and a t-statistic of at least 3, the threshold Harvey, Liu & Zhu (2016) propose for a new factor. Every run is kept, including runs a data correction replaced.

What a simple trading rule on the ranking would have done

A rule that holds the top 25, keeps each until it falls past rank 40, skip names flagged as value or growth traps, at most 6 in a sector, 5 basis points of cost a side, rebalanced monthly:

It trailed an equal-weighted index of the same stocks in every period, by most in the growth-led years of 2020 and 2021. Read any live record of the ranking next to an equal-weight fund.

The ranking's monthly correlation swings by about 0.12 from one month to the next, so any monthly ranking of large companies needs years of record before a realistic edge can be told apart from luck. That applies to this screener's live track record too. Here, the smallest difference the test could reliably see was about 0.039 over 2012–2019 and 0.066 over the held-out months.

What this means

Supported

Every factor the screener uses has a published source, and the Factor Lab reruns the studies behind its categories on public data. Every score on the dashboard can be rebuilt from its inputs. The testing is pre-registered and every run is kept.

Not supported

That the ranking beats the market, or that its top names will do better than the rest. On public data it has not been shown to predict returns, and the factor premiums it draws on were mostly weaker after their papers were published and weaker again among large companies.

So the screener is a structured, checkable way to look at a company across eight dimensions. It is not a forecast.

Limits of the test

  • It cannot include the analyst-based inputs (estimate revisions and forward earnings growth, about a sixth of the live weighting): no free history of them exists.
  • Companies that have since left the index are missing, which matters most in the earliest years.
  • Large companies only, rebalanced monthly, with costs approximated and no taxes.
