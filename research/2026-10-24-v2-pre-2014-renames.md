# Two free fixes to backtest v2's point-in-time record: pre-2014 renames, and predecessor filings

**2026-10-24, 13:00 session.** `OWNER_FOCUS.md`'s top open item (2026-10-20), sub-item 6, first
half: the two fixes `research/2026-10-20-free-survivorship-sources.md` section 6 found while
measuring the survivorship gap and deliberately did not make, because each is a data-version change
that stales every research-engine ledger row. They are done together here - one rebuild, one rerun -
as that item asks.

**Neither is survivorship.** Both are companies backtest v2 **has today** and drops anyway: one over
a symbol it cannot match, the other over filings it never fetched. The extended universe (that
item's second half, with `only-size-extended` to be registered first) is untouched and is the next
session's work.

**Nothing here decides a weight.** Under CLAUDE.md rule 5 no v2 number is decision-grade before
2027-02-11, and this note changes no weight, metric or threshold. What it changes is how much of the
2012-2026 record exists at all.

## 2. Fix 1: the renames the revisions cannot carry

`backtest_v2.Inputs.current_symbol` matches a month's member to today's record **by SEC registrant
number and by nothing else**, which is the right rule: a ticker that vanishes is not necessarily a
company that left, and only the CIK separates a rename from a removal (`universe_history.
parse_ticker_ciks`' docstring makes the case). But `parse_ticker_ciks` returns `{}` for every
revision before **2014-05**, because the constituents table had no CIK column until then. So a
company renamed before 2014-05 carries no registrant number anywhere in the membership history, and
the matcher has nothing to match on.

Each CIK below was resolved in `research/measurements/2026-10-20-survivorship-free-sources.py` by a
**unique** name match against SEC's own `cik-lookup-data.txt`, accepted only where the registrant
filed 10-Ks through the membership years (`classification[sym].cik_source`).

### The three admitted

| Symbol | Months | Registrant | Today | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BFB` | 6 | 14693 Brown-Forman | `BF-B` | The 2012 revisions write BF.B without the dot, so it normalises to `BFB` and never meets today's `BF-B`. Same registrant throughout; the note's float check agrees on 7 of 7 dates |
| `MHP` | 16 | 64040 McGraw-Hill | `SPGI` | Same registrant (exact name match to "S&P Global Inc."); no split or recapitalisation between 2005 and 2026 in `cache/splits.parquet` |
| `SAI` | 21 | 1336920 SAIC Inc | `LDOS` | Same registrant (exact match to "Leidos Holdings, Inc."); the 2013-09-30 ratio 0.405 is in `cache/splits.parquet`, which is what `pit_record.split_after` reconciles the filed share count against; the note's float check agrees on 9 of 9 dates |

For all three, the target symbol was **not itself a member in any affected month** (checked against
`universe_history.members_on` for each month), and `cache/v2_daily.parquet` carries its series from
2010-11-01, so the price history under it is the renamed company's own.

### The five refused, which are the rule rather than exceptions

- **`ACE` -> CB (48 months) and `TYC` -> JCI (56 months).** The target symbol was itself a member in
  **every** affected month - Chubb Corp and Johnson Controls Inc, both later absorbed by the
  registrant that now holds the symbol. One current record cannot stand for two different members,
  and the symbol's price history before the merger belongs to the other company: joining ACE's
  filings to CB's pre-2016 closes would mix two companies' price series, which is the exact defect
  `check_price_series_integrity` exists to catch. v2 already records the counterpart correctly -
  CB and JCI are marked `symbol_reused` in those months, not scored.
- **`CMCSK` -> CMCSA (3 months).** A second share class of a registrant already scored under CMCSA.
  Each company is scored once (`METHODOLOGY_CHANGELOG.md` 2026-10-21, the share-class decision);
  carrying it would put two lines in the ranking for one company, which is what that decision
  removed.
- **`PCS` -> TMUS (16 months).** The 2026-10-20 note's own identity check - SEC public float
  against the symbol's close - reports **MISMATCH**: median ratio 0.353, 2 of 9 dates agreeing.
  MetroPCS's 1:2 reverse split **and** $4.05 special dividend are not a clean split, so the filed
  share count cannot be reconciled with the adjusted price and the market value, which five
  valuation metrics divide by, would be wrong. `cache/splits.parquet` carries the 0.5 and not the
  dividend, which is why the check fails and why the split mechanism cannot rescue it.
- **`SAIC` (1 month).** The note resolves the symbol to Leidos's registrant (1336920) and takes its
  prices from today's `SAIC` - Science Applications International Corp, the company spun **out** in
  September 2013, CIK 1571123. Two different companies in one row. One month, and the ambiguity is
  real, so it stays out.

`universe_history.PRE_REVISION_CIKS` holds the three; `tests/test_v2_data_fixes.py` pins the closed
list, pins each refusal, and fails if the constant could ever overwrite a CIK the revisions supply.

## 3. Fix 2: each member's predecessor registrant

`data/sec/pit/facts.parquet` is fetched by **today's** CIK (`research/measurements/
2026-10-09-xbrl-point-in-time-census.py` resolves tickers through SEC's current ticker map). A
holding-company reorganisation or a redomicile creates a *new* registrant, so for every month before
it, v2 asks a CIK that had filed nothing. `record_at` then returns `no_filings`: **773 member-months
across 23 symbols**, measured, including the whole of 2012-2019 for Apache, BlackRock and Exxon.

Read off `facts.parquet` directly, the pattern is unmistakable - each symbol's filings begin the day
its current registrant started filing:

| Symbol | Earliest `filed` under today's CIK | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| XOM | 2026-08-03 | reorganised 2026; 96 of 96 months missing |
| BLK | 2024-11-06 | 2024 reorganisation; 96 of 96 |
| APA | 2021-05-07 | APA Corporation holding company, 2021; 96 of 96 |
| DIS | 2019-05-08 | TWDC Holdco, 2019; 88 |
| CI | 2019-02-28 | Cigna/Express Scripts holding company, Dec 2018; 85 |
| DD | 2017-11-06 | DowDuPont, Aug 2017; 68 |
| AVGO | 2018-06-14 | Broadcom's US redomicile, 2018; 49 |
| GOOG/GOOGL | 2015-10-29 | Alphabet, Oct 2015; 45 + 18 |
| MDT | 2015-02-27 | Medtronic plc, Jan 2015; 37 |

**These filings were public at the time.** They are the company's own, under the name it then had,
so joining them is the opposite of look-ahead - leaving them out is a hole in the point-in-time
record, and the only reason it exists is that the fetch was keyed by a symbol's *current*
registrant.

### The rule, which takes no list of companies

> For every ticker in today's universe, fetch every CIK the membership history
> (`ciks_by_month.json`) ever recorded for it that is not the CIK it carries today, and file those
> facts under today's ticker.

That is 22 pairs. `pit_record` then selects each concept by filed date exactly as before, so no
handover date has to be maintained: before the successor's first filing only the predecessor's
figures have been filed; afterwards the successor's restatement is the later filing and wins.

### What the rule gets wrong on its own, and the two bounds that fix it

The entity names the fetch prints are the warning. `IR`'s earlier CIK is **Trane Technologies plc**
and `Q`'s is **IQVIA Holdings** - companies that trade today under their own symbols - while `CB`'s
is Chubb Corp and `JCI`'s is Johnson Controls Inc, two companies absorbed by the registrant that now
holds the symbol. Their figures must never reach a scored record.

**The first bound I wrote was wrong, and measuring it is what showed that.** The draft refused any
pair whose predecessor kept filing after the successor started, on the theory that a real
reorganisation hands over cleanly. Measured on the 22 pairs it was wrong in **both** directions:

- it refused three genuine reorganisations - **Apache Corp (96 months), EIDP (68) and Dow Chemical**
  all still file as debt-only subsidiaries of their own holding companies, overlapping by 1,280,
  548 and 2,639 days;
- it admitted two different companies - **SanDisk Corp** and **Twenty-First Century Fox** - because
  each stopped filing before today's holder of its symbol was created.

**The cut alone is enough, and provably so.** Keep only rows filed **strictly before** today's
registrant's first filing. Because `pit_record` takes the latest filing, a kept row can only ever be
chosen for a month in which today's registrant had filed *nothing at all* - which is exactly the gap
being filled, and exactly the state in which v2 scores the name not at all today. Strictly before,
not on or before, so a same-day handover resolves to the successor (Alphabet and Google Inc both
filed on 2015-10-29; 34 of 820 rows dropped).

**One more bound: tenure.** The cut alone would let a registrant that lost the symbol years before
today's holder was created still be read - IQVIA held `Q` for three months in 2017 and filed
continuously until 2025. A registrant's filing can only inform a month in which it was that symbol's
registrant, so rows filed more than **120 days** (a quarter plus the 10-Q deadline, so the last
period it held the symbol is reported) after the last month the history named it are dropped too.
The effective bound is the earlier of the two.

Measured over the 22 pairs: **24,365 of 30,063 fetched facts kept**, for 18 tickers. `CB:20171` and
`JCI:53669` are cut to nothing - the two whose predecessor is a company today's registrant absorbed.
`DOW`, `Q` and `SNDK` are bounded by tenure; the rest by the cut. Two pairs could not be fetched at
all (`JKHY:1262439` and `URI:1215583` return HTTP errors - stale or wrong CIKs in a revision, the
kind the 2026-10-20 note names for Fox and Xerox) and are recorded as fetch failures rather than
silently absent.

`backtest_v2._load_facts` concatenates the side file and re-decides nothing;
`v2_experiments.DATA_SOURCES` records it, so a result produced with the predecessors joined cannot
share a data fingerprint with one produced without them. A missing side file is silently no
predecessors - a clean checkout has neither parquet.

## 5. What this does not fix

Of the 773 `no_filings` member-months, the general rule can only reach those whose predecessor the
membership history actually records - which means **2014-05 onward**, since that is where the CIK
column begins. Three groups stay:

- **Reorganisations before 2014-05.** ICE (22 months, the ICE Group holding company of Nov 2013) and
  ETN (10 months, Eaton Corp plc of Nov 2012) have only their current CIK in the history, so the
  rule cannot see a predecessor. They could be added to a constant the way the three renames were;
  32 member-months did not seem worth a hand-maintained list this session, and the choice is
  recorded here rather than left implicit.
- **Companies that genuinely had not filed yet.** ABBV (4 months), ALLE (4), NWSA (4), DWDP (2),
  EVRG (2), KHC, HPE, FTV, DOW (1 each) are spin-offs and merger entities whose first filing came
  after their first month in the index. There is nothing to join: the carve-out financials exist only
  inside the parent's registration statement, not as that registrant's XBRL facts. **This is not a
  defect** and should not be "fixed".
- **HST (25) and REG (18)**, whose facts begin in 2009 and 2010 - early enough - so their gap is a
  missing *concept* in the early filings, not a missing registrant. Unmeasured here; a separate
  question.

The 107 refused rename-months (ACE, TYC, CMCSK) are not fixable with free data and mostly should not
be: two are symbol handovers whose price series belong to another company, one is a share class the
methodology deliberately scores once.
