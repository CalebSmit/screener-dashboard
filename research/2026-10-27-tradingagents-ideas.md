# What TradingAgents has that the screener and the bot should take - and what they should not

**Date:** 2026-10-27 (owner-run). **Asked by the owner:** *"look into this repo to see if you can steal any
ideas from it for this tool and trading bot."*
**Read:** `TauricResearch/TradingAgents` at `c454491` (2026-10-27, v0.7.1): its README, `CHANGELOG.md`
(v0.5.2 to v0.7.1), `tradingagents/memory/settlement.py`, `memory/reflection.py`, `backtest.py`,
`portfolio.py`, `agents/rating.py`, `agents/post_screen.py` and the test list; and its paper, Xiao, Sun,
Luo & Wang, *TradingAgents: Multi-Agents LLM Financial Trading Framework*, arXiv:2412.20138 (v7, 2025;
the v7 HTML's author block also lists novelty names - cite the four above).

## 1. What it is

An LLM "trading firm" for **one ticker on one date**: four analyst agents (market/technicals,
sentiment from Reddit and StockTwits, news, fundamentals) write reports; a bull and a bear researcher
debate them; a research manager writes a plan; a trader proposes a trade; three risk debaters
(aggressive, neutral, conservative) argue it; a portfolio manager issues a five-tier rating (Buy,
Overweight, Hold, Underweight, Sell). Each decision is logged, later **settled** against a benchmark,
and turned into a two-to-four-sentence "lesson" that later runs read.

It is the opposite design to this screener: one stock, deep, narrative, non-deterministic, and
producing a rating; the screener ranks 500 stocks on published factors, deterministically, and never
emits a rating.

## 2. The evidence for the framework itself is not usable

The paper's whole test (section 5.1, Table 1) is **three stocks (AAPL, GOOGL, AMZN), January 1 to March
29, 2024**: cumulative returns of 23-27% and Sharpe ratios of **5.6 to 8.2**. That is about 60 trading
days of three correlated mega-caps; a Sharpe above 3 sustained for a year is rare for any strategy, and
three months cannot distinguish it from luck. More fundamentally, the language models it runs were
trained on text written **after** that window, so the agents may "know" what happened - the look-ahead
Glasserman & Lin (2023, *Assessing Look-Ahead Bias in Stock Return Predictions Generated by GPT
Sentiment Analysis*) measured in GPT sentiment scores. The repo's own changelog is a list of exactly
these leaks being closed one at a time (news read before it could have been traded, filings served
after the run date, an unpadded date comparing as later than a filing).

**Conclusion: nothing here is evidence that LLM agents pick stocks well, and nothing in this note
proposes putting an LLM's opinion into a score, a rank or an order.** That is CLAUDE.md's mandate
(evidence before methodology) and settled row "ctx" (context is shown beside the score, never in it).
What is worth taking is the engineering discipline the project learned the hard way.

## 3. Take: a decision is scored from the first close *after* it could have been acted on

TradingAgents #1518 / #1539 / #1548 (v0.7.0-0.7.1): a decision dated *d* is entered at the first close
after *d*, never on a bar still trading, and one made before a long closure settles when trading resumes.

**The screener's track record already does this** (`track_record.py`: entry at the first trading day
on or after the run date). **The live evidence base does not, on market holidays.**
`improvement_engine.compute_forward_returns` enters each snapshot at `_nearest_price(prices, run_date)`
- the nearest close **in either direction**, ties to the earlier. The data loop runs Monday to Friday,
holidays included (`_is_market_day` checks the weekday only), and a 02:00 run is built from the
previous session's close. So on a holiday whose previous close is at least as near as its next one, the
entry is **the close the ranking was computed from, before it was published** - a small look-ahead in
`live_ic_history.csv`, the file the engine's gates read.

Measured on the committed history: 65 snapshot dates, **one on an NYSE holiday (2026-09-07, Labor
Day)**, and it escaped by luck - Friday is three days back, Tuesday one day forward. The next two do
not: **2026-11-26 (Thanksgiving, Thursday)** ties Wednesday against Friday and takes Wednesday;
**2026-12-25 (Friday)** takes Thursday 12-24.

**Proposal status: OPEN** - enter each snapshot at the first close **on or after** its run date (the
rule `_horizon_end_close` already applies to the end of a horizon, and `track_record.py` to entry), with
a regression test on a Thanksgiving-shaped series, before 2026-11-26. It changes how a comparison is
measured, not a weight, so it is recorded in `METHODOLOGY_CHANGELOG.md` (the 2026-10-16 horizon-end
entry is the precedent) and leaves the 2026-09-07 rows unchanged (their nearest close is already the
next one).

## 4. Take, for the bot: score every swap, not just the book

TradingAgents settles **every decision** at a fixed horizon against a benchmark and keeps the record
separate from any portfolio simulation (`backtest.py`: "it evaluates decision quality. It is not a
portfolio simulator, and must not grow one").

The bot has the portfolio half (NAV, attribution, closed trades) but not the decision half. Each
rebalance is a set of **swaps**: sell A, buy B. The question the hold band (screener priority 5
residual; bot ROADMAP phase 4 item 7) needs answered is whether those swaps add value after costs.
A swap ledger answers it directly: for each swap, B's return minus A's from the first close after the
order to 5, 21 and 63 sessions later, net of the measured round-trip cost (ROADMAP 3.5's arrival
cost). Summed, it is the value of turnover itself. Novy-Marx & Velikov (2016, *A Taxonomy of Anomalies
and Their Trading Costs*) is the reason it matters: cost-mitigating buffers help exactly when the
marginal swap is worth less than its cost.

**Proposal status: OPEN** - a `swaps.csv` in the bot's journal written at each rebalance, settled by
later runs, shown on the dashboard's tear sheet with the same "not decided under N observations" rule
as Sharpe (ROADMAP 3.1). Bot repo, owner-run (screener nightlies do not work there). It is a
measurement, never an input to what the bot trades.

## 5. Take, for the page: the case against, in the engine's own numbers

The bull/bear debate is the framework's most-copied idea. Its useful core needs no LLM: before
deciding, read the strongest case **against** as carefully as the case for. The drilldown already has
"Why it ranks here" (the case for) and pieces of the case against scattered across "Read with care",
"Before you decide", the trap flags, input churn and the filing check.

**Proposal status: DECIDED 2026-10-27** - accepted by the owner ("Yes make all changes necessary") and
built the same day: claim `drilldown.case_against`, `tests/test_case_against.py`, described in
`plan/dashboard-inventory.md`. "The review window" was read as Reporting soon's 14 days (the configured
review cadence is quarterly, which would hold nearly every report), and "analyst targets below the price"
as the mean target below it (the summary's own sentence). The proposal as written: one "The case against" block at the top of the drilldown that only
**collects** what the page already publishes for that stock: its weakest weighted categories, any trap
or caveat flag, insider net selling, price below its 200-day average, an earnings date inside the
review window, analyst targets below the price, and inputs that disagree with the company's filing.
Each line links to its section. No new number, no new threshold, no verdict, no "sell". Every sentence
goes through `claims.py` like the summary's. Owner product call, then a Tuesday build.

## 6. Take as habits, not features

- **"REVIEW", not a silent default** (`rating.py`, #1170): output that cannot be read is flagged, never
  turned into a neutral value. The bot already declines a stale or unverified `signals.json`; keep
  that shape for anything new.
- **Reports name what produced them** (v0.5.2): already true here (`kpis.run_id`, `git_sha`,
  `raw_sha256`, `methodology_version`).
- **Three states for a book: positions, flat, unknown** (`portfolio.py`): "not provided" is not "flat".
  Worth keeping in mind if My Holdings ever reads a broker export.
- **Date strings are compared only after parsing** (#1488: `2024-7-5` compared as later than
  `2024-07-30`). Every date this project compares comes from its own ISO writers; no defect found,
  but a new free-text date input should be parsed, never string-compared.

## 7. Leave

- **LLM analysts and debates as a signal.** No usable evidence (section 2), non-deterministic,
  unexplainable to an investment club, and they break "the page shows the engine's numbers". Narrative
  belongs where the bot repo already keeps it: written research briefs (`briefs/`) that a human reads.
- **Reddit / StockTwits sentiment.** The paper's own inputs; no peer-reviewed large-company edge net of
  costs that this project could reproduce on free data, and paid or rate-limited APIs.
- **Prediction-market odds** (Polymarket) as macro context. Free, but FRED already carries the
  backdrop, and the odds would be one more context line with no record.
- **A five-tier rating.** A recommendation by another name - the Model Portfolio decision (2026-08-26).
