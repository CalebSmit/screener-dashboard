# Factor Lab experiment 15: Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen's 153 factors, and the mega caps

**Date:** 2026-10-20. **Kind:** a data-source evaluation, a lab rerun, and what both say about the
screener. **Not a proposal.** Nothing here changes a weight or a metric; the lab never justifies a
screener parameter by itself (`plan/factor-lab.md`, rule 1, and "From the lab to the weights").

## 1. Why this source

The owner asked on 2026-10-20 whether other free data could widen the lab. `plan/factor-lab.md`
("Free data to evaluate") named two candidates; this note is the first. Jensen, Kelly & Pedersen
(2023) publish 153 US factors built one uniform way, the same characteristics **inside each NYSE
size group**, and 92 other countries. The size groups matter most here: experiments 8 and 9 answer
"do the factors hold among large companies?" with the only public construction the lab had
(Fama-French 2x3 and 5x5 portfolios, value-weighted, big half and largest fifth). This library is a
second, independent construction of the same question, and its "mega" group (above the NYSE 80th
percentile, about 430 companies in 2025) is close to the companies the screener ranks.

## 2. Terms and access - checked at the source

**Access.** Everything the lab needs downloads anonymously over plain HTTPS from the authors' public
S3 bucket, with no account, payment, bot check or CAPTCHA:

- The download page (`jkpfactors.com/data`) builds its links in its own script: factors at
  `https://jkpfactors-data.s3.amazonaws.com/public/[usa]_[all_factors]_[monthly]_[vw_cap].zip`
  (brackets URL-encoded), size-group factors at `.../public/factor/[usa]_[<factor>]_[mega].zip`
  (one file per factor and group; there is no bulk size-group file - a bulk request returns 403),
  and a manifest at `.../public/availability.json`.
- The tidyfinance R package's `download_data_jkp()` documents the same bucket and key scheme.
- The factor-details spreadsheet (each factor's original paper, its in-sample period, its sign and
  whether the original paper found it significant) and the theme labels are in the authors' code
  repository, `github.com/bkelly-lab/jkp-data`, `src/jkp/data/resources/`.
- **Stock-level** characteristics are **not** free: the page says they need a WRDS subscription.
  The lab uses only the portfolio returns.

`factor_lab.fetch_jkp` downloads 310 files (the all-factors file, the market, the two metadata files,
and 153 x 2 size-group files) to `data/lab/jkp/` (gitignored, never committed). It runs only under
`python factor_lab.py --fetch`; no test touches the network.

**Licence.** The data page's footer states "Data is licensed under CC BY-NC 4.0" and "Analysis code
is available under the MIT License"; the repository's `DATA_LICENSE` says the same, names the
copyright holders (Theis Ingerslev Jensen, Bryan T. Kelly, Lasse Heje Pedersen, 2023) and asks for
the paper to be cited. What CC BY-NC 4.0 permits, from the licence text:

- **Share** (copy and redistribute) and **adapt** (remix, transform, build upon), in any medium;
- **on condition of attribution**: credit, a link to the licence, and a statement of what was
  changed, in a way that does not suggest endorsement;
- **NonCommercial**: not for commercial purposes;
- **no additional restrictions**: no terms or technical measures that stop others doing what the
  licence allows.

**How the lab complies.** The page is public, free and educational. `lab_data.js` publishes derived
series (rounded to a basis point; the size-group series re-signed), and the page footer carries the
data's name, the paper, "Licensed CC BY-NC 4.0" with a link to the licence, and a "Changed here"
sentence (`attribution_jkp`; `tests/test_factor_lab.py::test_the_jkp_data_is_published_whole_and_attributed`).

**What it does not permit - the owner should know.** The memory file records that real money is the
eventual goal (`CalebSmit/screener-bot`; "my hedge fund/real trading bot"). **This data may not be
used commercially.** It is fine where it is: a public teaching page that the screener's code never
reads (rule 1; `test_no_scoring_module_reads_the_lab`). It must never become an input to a
commercial product or a fund's process. The lab's route to a weight already runs through written
research rather than through data, which keeps that line clean; keep it that way.

## 3. The paper, and where each figure came from

Theis Ingerslev Jensen, Bryan Kelly and Lasse Heje Pedersen, "Is there a replication crisis in
finance?", *Journal of Finance* 78(5), 2465-2518 (2023), doi:10.1111/jofi.13249. Read with `pypdf`
from the published-article PDF the authors keep in their code repository (`documentation/`), 54
pages; every figure below is in its text layer (none read off a chart).

| Figure | Paper | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Share of all 153 US factors with a significant average return | 55.6% | Figure 1, 2nd bar; p. 2467 |
| The same, among the 119 that their own papers found significant | 61.3% | Figure 1, 3rd bar; p. 2467 |
| The 119: share with a significant CAPM alpha | 82.4% (98 of 119) | Figure 1, 4th bar, p. 2468; p. 2489 |
| Mega caps: share of the 119 (empirical Bayes) | 77.3% | Figure 5A; p. 2491 |
| Large caps: share of the 119 (empirical Bayes) | 79.8% | Figure 5A; p. 2491 |
| Average alpha inside the original samples (scaled to 10% idiosyncratic vol) | 0.49% a month | p. 2494 |
| Average alpha after them | 0.26% a month | p. 2494 |
| Share positive before / after / both | 82.6% / 83.3% / 87.4% | p. 2495; Figure 8 caption p. 2496 (102, 115, 119 factors) |

Construction (pp. 2486-2489): terciles on non-micro stocks, capped value weights (market equity
winsorized at the NYSE 80th percentile), top minus bottom tercile signed by the original paper,
accounting data lagged four months, US sample from CRSP (1926 on) to 2020. "Replicates" means the
t-statistic clears 1.96 in the original paper's direction.

**What the paper does not say, or the lab cannot do:** the standard errors are the diagonal of a
bootstrapped, block-equicorrelated covariance of alphas (Appendix B, pp. 2514-2515) - the lab uses
each factor's own OLS error; the size-group rates are empirical-Bayes posteriors - the lab counts
OLS; the text does not say how its size-group factors are weighted (the public files weight by
rank on the characteristic, per `aux_functions.py` in the authors' code); it does not say which
factors the 0.49% and 0.26% averages are taken over (the lab uses Figure 8 Panel B's set). Not
attempted: the multiple-testing rate (75.6%, which needs the paper's bootstrap critical value of
2.7), the posteriors themselves, the GLS slopes of Figure 8, and the small/micro/nano rates.

## 4. Tolerances, written before the comparison

Committed on their own in `438a7f9`, after the files were downloaded and parsed for their shape and
before any return was computed on: 5.0 points a rate on all stocks (one factor is 0.84 points of
119; five points is about six factors crossing 1.96 either way); 8.0 for the size groups (the
estimator differs as well); 0.06% a month for the two average alphas; 5.0 for the shares positive.
Plan rule 8, from the record: a result of no effect - 2.5% clearing 1.96 by chance, half the factors
positive, zero alpha - matches **none** of the ten (`null_rows` gained `NULL_CHANCE` for shares).

## 6. Reading it

**Among mega caps the record holds almost as well as across all stocks - through 2020.** The
t-statistics of the 119 factors' alphas in all stocks and in mega caps correlate at 0.95, and 90 of
the 119 clear the bar among mega caps alone.

**Since 2014 it mostly does not clear the bar, and that is mostly a statement about power.** Of the
119, among mega caps, 9 have a significant CAPM alpha since 2014 (to December 2025); 89 of 119 have a
*positive* alpha. Since 2021: 13 significant, 91 positive. Twelve years is too short for an alpha of
this size to clear 1.96 (the lab's standing reading rule: under twenty years and inside 2 is "not
confirmed", not "gone"). The average alpha among mega caps was 0.26% a month to 2020 and 0.26% since
2021 - no decay in the level over that five-year stretch, which is not significant either way.

**The measures the screener weights, inside the mega caps** (rank-weighted, signed; % a month,
t-statistic of the raw average; reproducible from `lab_data.js` with `factor_lab.jkp_window` and `jkp_capm`):

| Factor (JKP name) | To 2020 | Since 2000 | Since 2014 | Screener |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Book to market (`be_me`) | +0.04 (0.3) | +0.14 (0.6) | -0.30 (-1.0) | book value carries weight only for banks |
| Earnings yield (`ni_me`) | +0.27 (2.2) | +0.47 (1.8) | -0.09 (-0.3) | Valuation |
| Free-cash-flow yield (`fcf_me`) | +0.31 (3.5) | +0.52 (2.8) | +0.25 (1.1) | Valuation's heaviest metric |
| EBITDA to EV (`ebitda_mev`) | +0.31 (2.5) | +0.58 (2.1) | -0.04 (-0.1) | Valuation |
| Momentum 12-1 (`ret_12_1`) | +0.50 (3.6) | +0.26 (0.9) | +0.25 (0.9) | Momentum |
| Gross profit to assets (`gp_at`) | +0.29 (3.3) | +0.30 (2.0) | +0.26 (1.1) | Quality |
| Operating profit to book (`ope_be`) | +0.26 (3.2) | +0.39 (2.6) | +0.19 (1.3) | Quality |
| Asset growth (`at_gr1`) | +0.15 (1.5) | +0.29 (1.4) | -0.06 (-0.3) | Investment |

Four things this second construction says, each against what the lab already had:

1. **Value among the largest companies lives in earnings and cash flow, not book value** - the same
   conclusion as `research/2026-10-12-value-and-momentum-among-large-companies.md` (Israel &
   Moskowitz 2013; Asness et al. 2015) and experiment 9, now on an independent construction:
   book-to-market never clears the bar among mega caps; free-cash-flow yield does, in every period
   to 2020 and since 2000.
2. **Free-cash-flow yield among mega caps looks better here than cash-flow yield did in experiment
   11.** There, by market value across the whole market, the cash-flow-yield spread was about zero
   since 1990. Two differences, both towards the screener's metric: JKP's measure subtracts capital
   spending (the French sort's does not), and it ranks within the mega caps with rank weights
   rather than letting the very largest names dominate. This is a lead for a research note on the
   FCF-yield metric, not a reason for anything.
3. **Momentum's flat stretch since 2000 appears here too** (+0.26, t 0.9), matching experiments 8
   and 9 and the Momentum note: lab and literature still disagree, and the weight stays.
4. **Profitability is again the measure that holds best among large companies since 2000**
   (`ope_be` t 2.6, `gp_at` t 2.0), as experiment 8 found on the French portfolios.

None of this is evidence for a weight (the lab's portfolios are long-short, cost-free, one
characteristic at a time across a size group; the screener combines 28 metrics within sectors and
holds the top). It is evidence about the published research, and the four points agree with what
the lab and the research notes had already found.

## 7. What this data could do next

- The **"cannot be rerun on free data"** row is not closed by this library: Piotroski, Beneish and
  analyst revisions are not among its 153 factors (it excludes I/B/E/S data, p. 2487). That is the
  second candidate's job (Chen & Zimmermann's Open Source Asset Pricing, being evaluated in
  parallel).
- **Experiments 8 and 9 on this construction.** The mega- and large-cap files give value,
  momentum, profitability and investment within each group, back to the 1950s. A "split" record
  reading them would set the two constructions side by side.
- **Outside the US.** The same factors for 92 countries; the lab has no non-US evidence at all.
- **The payload.** Three universes of 153 factors take `lab_data.js` from 1.17 MB to about 2.9 MB
  (whole basis points). If the lab grows further, split the JKP series into their own file loaded
  only by this experiment.

quantity. One thing a careful reader may notice, which is **the paper's, not a transcription
error**: 82.6% is not a whole number of factors out of 102 (84/102 = 82.4, 85/102 = 83.3), and 83.3%
is not one out of 115 (95/115 = 82.6, 96/115 = 83.5). The two percentages fit the caption's counts
only the other way round - 85 of 102 before, 95 of 115 after - so the text has probably swapped
"pre" and "post", or computed them over a set it does not name. The lab compares the figures as the
paper prints them, which is right; read the other way, the lab's 84/102 and 96/115 would differ by
one factor each (-1.0 and +0.9 points), well within 5.0 either way. The text also says "positive
returns" where Figure 8 plots alphas; the lab tests the alpha, which is what the figure shows.
