**How some screens are built here** (`construction_note`):

- **only-size** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles): the screen favours companies that are small for their sector. Fama & French (1992) sort the whole market by size.
- **piotroski-only** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles): the F-score is compared with sector peers. Piotroski (2000) applies it across the market, among high book-to-market firms.
- **profitability-and-value** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles). The value half is earnings yield, where Novy-Marx (2013) uses book-to-market; his hedge comes from profitability's negative correlation with book-to-market. Here earnings yield and gross profit to assets have an average monthly within-sector rank correlation of +0.045 over 176 months (research/measurements/2026-10-16-stocks-ranked-per-config.json), so the screen does not carry that hedge.
- **fcf-yield-only** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles): free-cash-flow yield against sector peers, which is how the screener's Valuation category uses it.
- **momentum-12-1-only** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles): a stock's 12-1 month return against its sector peers, which removes the industry component of momentum (Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999). Jegadeesh & Titman (1993) rank across the whole market.
- **low-volatility-only** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles): volatility against sector peers. Ang et al. (2006) and Frazzini & Pedersen (2014) sort across the whole market.
- **52-week-high** - Ranked within sector, as the engine ranks every metric (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles): nearness to the 52-week high against sector peers. George & Hwang (2004) rank across the whole market.
- **drop-revenue-growth** - Two differences from the cited papers and one from the live screener, all stated rather than assumed. (1) Both papers test the Lakonishok-Shleifer-Vishny FIVE-YEAR weighted rank of sales growth; this metric is one year - the latest quarter against the same quarter a year earlier (changelog 2026-10-09). The established null is therefore about the longer-horizon construction, and this experiment is what tests the one-year one here. (2) Both papers rank across the market; the engine ranks every metric within the stock's GICS sector (factor_engine.compute_sector_percentiles), so the screen is relative to sector peers. (3) In this configuration forward_eps_growth has no free history and carries no weight, so Growth is revenue_growth 25 / revenue_cagr_3yr 15 / sustainable_growth 15 and this leg is 5.91 composite points rather than the live 3.25; the engine renormalises the two that remain, so the category keeps its 13 points and its role as the composite's counterweight (research/2026-10-12-growth-category.md section 10). This is deliberately NOT drop-growth, which removes the counterweight itself and was already run (-0.003, t -0.49).


## Controls - can this test see a signal at all?

A control holds the weighting fixed and changes the data, so its answer is known to be large before it runs. It is **not** an alternative anyone could choose - its data does not exist at the moment a real decision would be made - and it is **not** counted in the multiple-testing bar. It is the evidence that the verdicts above mean *there was nothing to find* rather than *this test cannot see anything*.

What would count as a failure, written before the run: The difference does not clear p < 0.05/N and |t| >= 3 over the 80 months. That would NOT be a finding about the screener: it would mean this design cannot see a signal of the size look-ahead manufactures, and every "not distinguishable from the baseline" verdict in the ledger - and the research page's sentence about the test seeing a signal - would have to be re-read as uninformative until the cause was found. It is a defect to investigate, not a result to publish.

How it is built here, against the paper it cites: How this differs from the paper, every difference stated because each one makes the stop fire sooner, later or not at all. (1) The paper triggers on the day's LOWEST price (eq. 1, p. 5); cache/v2_daily.parquet carries no intraday high or low, so the trigger here is the day's adjusted close - a stop that fires less often than the paper's, and only on falls that hold to the close. (2) The paper does not sell on the trigger day: it sells on the following day at a limit price LP_D = (1-L/2)(1-L)P0 - 85.5% of P0 at L = 10% - or at that day's close if the whole day trades below it, and keeps the stock if the day's low stays above it (p. 5-6, the L/2 threshold "to make sure that the initial drop is genuine"). Here the sale is at the triggering close, so this stop exits sooner and higher than the paper's and also exits names the paper's threshold would have kept. (3) The paper invests the proceeds in T-bills for the rest of the month; cash earns nothing in this harness everywhere, so the stop is charged that lost yield. (4) The paper's portfolio is long-short six-month momentum deciles over all CRSP common stocks with a one-month gap, prices over $5 and the smallest NYSE size decile excluded; only the sell-side stop applies here, to the long side of a 25-name eight-category screen of S&P 500 members.
